Onward Onward Onward Onward Onward

(I have a master’s now)

August 25, 2026

This week I am finding myself catching my breath after the full tilt sprint that was a one year master’s in a technical field. Aand, for as much as it may seem like one year isn’t enough to really get a masters, I actually feel like I have a lot of hard skills to show for it. The folks at Northwestern really know their stuff- and they do the absolute best job possible at allowing creativity, freedom, and encouragement to rule each moment of the year. I went from a sort of confused and burnt out composition student who felt like he should probably be doing more with electronics to a full on audio engineer and as much of a programmer as an artist needs to be (maybe except for C++... I don’t wanna open that can of worms yet…).

So What Did I Actually Do For The Past Twelve Months?

Well, I got to focus on interactive art, computer code, video game sound design, and writing music where it was needed. It was really cool to feel like I never wrote any music all year, and then suddenly arrive at events like our academic symposium and realize that I had contributed musically to like four or five other projects on top of my own symposium, and then for a similar thing to happen at the final thesis presentations. I felt like I was barely writing anything, but I think in reality I was settling into this workflow where opportunities to fill sonic space made it wayyyy easier to write than trying to create the space itself. Of course, I found myself producing strange little pieces of music here and there just for music’s sake, but the race to learn programming took most of my time and energy and also brainspace normally spent worrying if I was writing enough music.

Speaking of working on other people’s projects, I want to mention a couple dope ass classmates and friends who I got to learn so much from while also collaborating with on awesome projects.

Daniel McGrath and I spent a tonnnnnn of time together this year, being the two horn players in the program. And, to make it even more perfect we both want so badly to go into video games. We got to work on 4 big projects this year:

  • Bitcrush music and code competition
  • A brasstracks/nine sparks riots/sam gendel/louis cole brass EP
  • Project Reign, a prototype of a game for Progression Mechanics Game Jam
  • Daniel’s Symposium DUNKR and thesis PSYCHE, which I did the music for

Daniel is an awesome sound designer who somehow pulled me out from the rock I was living under and forced me to get hip to all the new sample libraries and VSTs, and the existence of Brasstracks. His spatial audio work has been super inspiring too- we took a class in spatial audio this spring and his implementation in the video game engine is all stuff that is at the top of my own to-do list now.

Cole Savo and I also spent a ton of time together mainly because that game jam project was his creative baby. Him, Daniel, and I plunged into a game jam as three sound designers and only about a week in thought “oh shit we probably need a dedicated programmer and artist for this don’t we” and somehow (thanks to Colin Schilf) pulled through with a function rhythm-combat game with two tracks and a functional combat loop which uses on our audio engine to run rhythmically at variable tempos. I also got to mix a bunch of Cole’s music for our mixing/mastering class, and while the mixes are still getting reworked the spatial audio version of Disintegrate is something I hope will be hitting streaming sooner rather than later.

I want to give a huge shoutout to Samuel Osorio who was willing to hand Daniel and I all his reject drafts of songs so that we could put as much brass in them as we wanted. Sam’s production is top notch, and his projects with Daisy’s seed, Max/MSP, and audio electronics were super awesome to see unfold. Those tracks are also hopefully going to hit youtube sooner rather than later- pending give or take 100 more layers of trombones /jk.

I also wanted to just throw in a quick thank you to Mason Blair for BUILDING A RIBBON MIC and then letting me multitrack trombones on it to demonstrate. King.

Also, lastly but DEFINITELY not leastly, shoutout to Joe Heck and Mathilde Marsot, who both had me involved in their own respective projects as a studio musician. I need to keep the playing up and artists like them have been awesome to work with.

Collaborations aside, I got to produce two video game music designs/implimentations and get feedback from video game industry professionals as a part of our video game sound design class. While they are definitely not ready to show to the world, I am super pumped to continue with in-engine designs once I finish my current web design project. For my academic capstone, I chose to develop interactive web audio modules using RNBO and Javascript. I did one small module, my website’s homepage mouse-position granular synthesizer, and a big project: “TryAllYouWant,” a larger scale interactive experience more like a video game. I ran into a lot of roadblocks but found that this pipeline is a lot more intuitive than just coding in the WebAudioAPI is, and there are a handful of great resources that I link to in my article about the project.

I also got the chance to finalize some research from my undergraduate study on field recordings and contemporary music as a part of the Lithuanian Composer’s Union’s 2025 “Principles of Composing” conference. It was super exciting to be a part of that event.

Now, I am hitting the ground running (or at least, maybe like jogging) with a couple of exciting projects. “Moreose” is an ASCII art inspired audio-reactive video game that right now is more audio than game, but that I am actively working on in an effort to work completely with vanilla JS for web. Daniel and I are working on the brass EP we recorded this fall, continuing with production and some tracking, and mixing/mastering (and hopefully some videos to go with). I am working on finalizing the article that the LCU will publish as a part of their 2025 journal, and I am continuing to work with the Creative Audio Archive as a transfer engineer. I also have a couple scores in my back pocket that will be heard alongside films written by Ishita Mishra, currently in the post-production stage.
